Overview: Narrative Arc Across Two Side Quests
These two side quests are designed to work as a narrative duo , reflecting the player’s choices
across time and reinforcing key themes of FOQUES , such as community, trust, and
consequence. Both quests are set in the forests of Northern Canada and incorporate stealth,
puzzles, environmental storytelling, and subtle emotional impact without direct combat.

Side Quest 1 – “Pacts Beneath the Snow”
Core Theme:
“The enemy of my enemy is my friend.”
Narrative Summary:
While crossing a remote snowy glade, Kari encounters Raaz , a wounded red lynx—one of her
natural predators—caught in an old human trap. As infected creatures close in, Raaz swallows
his pride and begs for help. The player must decide: help an enemy or leave him to his fate.
This decision influences not only the short-term gameplay but also future quests. Trust and
survival intertwine here as Kari must weigh morality against instinct.
Narrative Purpose:
● Establish a complex moral dilemma early on.
● Explore reluctant alliances and natural hierarchy reversal.
● Lay the groundwork for consequence-based storytelling in the second quest.
Key Character:

● Raaz – A proud, sarcastic lynx unwilling to show weakness. If saved, he becomes a
temporary ally and source of guidance.
Gameplay Elements:
● Environmental puzzle to release the trap.
● Tension build-up via proximity to infected creatures.
● Possible stealth section depending on the player's method.
Branching Outcomes:
Choice Result
Help Raaz He survives, shares valuable survival tips, may reappear later (as an ally or
a foe).
Abandon
him
He dies or becomes corrupted by The Stain—Kari will face the
consequences later.

Side Quest 2 – “Echoes in the Gorge”
Core Theme:
“Everything returns eventually.” (The Butterfly Effect)
Narrative Summary:
As Kari ventures through a narrow gorge overgrown with infected plants, she begins to
hallucinate. The Stain’s spores trigger vivid visions of the past and imagined futures—reflecting
previous player choices and unresolved guilt.
Some visions are comforting. Others, horrifying. If Raaz was abandoned, he appears corrupted
and aggressive. If saved, he returns as a mental anchor, guiding Kari through the madness.
This is a psychological horror experience—light on objectives, heavy on atmosphere and
emotional weight.
Narrative Purpose:

● Reflect on the player’s journey and moral path.
● Use hallucinations as narrative feedback.
● Subvert expectations by removing immediate reward.
Vision Characters:
● Infected or spectral versions of NPCs from earlier quests.
● A dark, monstrous version of Kari if the player made selfish choices.
● Raaz (either corrupted or protective).
Gameplay Elements:
● Puzzle navigation guided by fragmented visions.
● Disorienting movement/environment shifting mechanics.
● Sound and light cues to indicate reality vs hallucination.
Branching Outcomes:
Player's Past Action Hallucination Outcome
Saved Raaz He helps Kari resist the illusions and points to the exit.
Abandoned Raaz A corrupted version of him chases or haunts Kari.

Quest 1 – “Pacts Beneath the Snow” (Narrative Draft)
The snow crunched under Kari’s paws, each step sinking into the soft ice that covered the
remnants of an old forgotten path. Whisker circled above her head, occasionally swooping low
enough for his talons to graze the snow.
"I can’t stand this smell," said the owl, shaking his feathers before gaining height again. "The
corruption seeps even into places where nothing living remains."
Kari sniffed the air. Something was wrong—there was a metallic tang, almost like rusted iron,
mixed with the sour scent of decay.
"Do you think the Stain has reached this place too?" she asked, her voice barely above a
whisper, as if afraid of waking something dormant.
"The Stain reaches everywhere sooner or later," Whisker replied. "But... There's still something
here. I can feel it."
They moved forward until they reached the outskirts of an old, abandoned village. The wooden
cabins, blackened by time and fire, stood like skeletal remains. Empty windows, broken glass, a

rusted iron bell hanging askew from a tower that no longer rang. The silence was so dense that
each step felt like it shattered it.
As they explored the ruins, a sound tore through the stillness. It wasn’t the howl of a wolf or the
caw of a crow. It was a lament—a low, broken sound, filled with both pain and fury. Kari tensed,
her ears swiveling in all directions.
"Did you hear that?" she whispered.
"Yes... and I don’t like it," Whisker answered, landing on a snow-covered roof. "It’s coming from
over there."
They followed the sound to a narrow street buried under debris. Between two collapsed houses,
they saw the source: an old iron trap, rusted yet cruelly effective, clamped tightly around the leg
of a red lynx. Its mottled fur was matted with blood, and the snow beneath it was a canvas of
bright crimson. Kari froze, her heartbeat quickening. The lynx raised its head, locking eyes with
her.
There was an instant of silence, as though the world itself had stopped. Kari instinctively
stepped back, her fur bristling.
"It’s a lynx..." she muttered.
"And it’s trapped. Don’t be afraid, Kari," Whisker said in a firm tone, though his feathers were
ruffled. "It can’t hurt you."
Despite its pain, the lynx lifted its head with an aura of superiority, studying Kari as if she were
prey. It tried to lunge forward—a reflex of dominance—but a guttural cry of pain stopped it short.
Kari recognized him immediately.
"Raaz..." she whispered, her voice tight.
Whisker tilted his head.
"You know him?"
"Not personally... but I’ve heard stories," Kari replied, never breaking eye contact. "He... he
killed one of mine. My sister."
Raaz met her gaze with a bitter smirk.
"Those are just memories, fox. Will you let the past decide if I live or die?"
A deep roar echoed from the forest, shattering the moment. It was a sound too guttural, too
unnatural. The metallic stench of the Stain began to rise, seeping into the air like poison.
Raaz’s eyes softened for the first time.
"Help me... or we’ll all be devoured."

Kari hesitated. Her instincts screamed to run, but compassion—or perhaps fear of leaving him
to a worse fate—fought to surface. Whisker, however, was resolute.
"Don’t do it, Kari. He’s a killer. He doesn’t deserve your help."
Raaz growled, his voice low but sharp.
"And who are you to decide, bird? Without me, you won’t survive what’s coming."
Time was running out. Kari took a deep breath and made her choice.
If Kari helps Raaz:
Without a word, Kari scouted the ruins, finding a rusted metal rod. She jammed it into the hinge
of the trap, straining with all her strength to pry it open. Raaz watched, tense and silent, while
Whisker muttered darkly from atop a broken post.
"You’ll regret this," the owl grumbled.
With a sharp metallic snap, the trap sprang open. Raaz growled in relief, rising shakily to his
feet, his golden eyes glinting.
"I don’t need your pity," he said, stepping forward. "But... I owe you."
Whisker swooped down, blocking his path. Raaz’s paw lashed out with startling speed, his
claws grazing the owl’s wing and tearing out feathers, leaving a streak of blood.
"Whisker!" cried Kari, rushing to shield her companion.
Raaz backed away, calmer now.
"Consider that a reminder of what I am. Don’t follow me."
The lynx disappeared into the ruins, leaving Kari to cradle Whisker.
"We need help," the owl whispered hoarsely.
Kari’s gaze shifted to the distant gorge. There, perhaps, they would find a cure.
If Kari abandons Raaz:
Kari took a step back, shaking her head.
"I’m sorry. I can’t."
Raaz stared at her in burning silence, his eyes filled with rage and disbelief.
"You’re doing the right thing," Whisker said coldly. "He doesn’t deserve to live."

The roars from the forest grew louder. As they fled, the last sound they heard was Raaz’s
agonized cry as the Stain’s creatures closed in. Kari didn’t dare look back.
Quest 2 – “Echoes in the Gorge” (Narrative Draft)
The forest opened into a frozen void. Kari stood on the edge of the gorge, her paws sinking
slightly into the brittle snow. The gorge itself stretched endlessly, its surface cracked like a
spider’s web. What should have been pure white and blue glistened instead with unnatural veins
of green and violet—the signature infection of the Stain. It pulsed faintly beneath the ice, like
something alive, something watching.
Whisker landed beside her with a soft thud, his talons gripping the ice.
"We need to cross," he said quietly, his voice low and tense. "But the spores here are... thicker.
Don’t breathe too deep."
Kari nodded, but as she inhaled, a sharp bitterness coated her throat. The plants at the gorge’s
edges were wrong—blackened stalks twisting into strange spirals, as if clawing at the air. A fine
mist drifted across the chasm, glittering faintly under the pale light.
She took her first step, and the world shifted.
If Kari helped Raaz:
The cold air turned heavy, pressing against her chest. Kari blinked and saw Whisker ahead of
her—yet something was wrong. His feathers were darkened, slick with tar-like tendrils of the
Stain, his beak twisted, his eyes glowing a sickly green.
"Whisker...?" Kari’s voice trembled.
The infected image of her friend let out a scream—sharp, hollow, nothing like the Whisker she
knew. Kari stumbled backward. Her paws slipped on the ice, and the world bent around her, the
gorge stretching and spiraling like a nightmare.
"Kari," a voice whispered behind her—Whisker’s real voice, faint and pained.
She turned to see him hunched, his wing bloodied from Raaz’s earlier strike.
"It’s not real. Look at me... I’m still here. Don’t lose yourself."
Kari’s heart pounded. She took a shaky breath and focused on Whisker’s voice. Slowly, the
hallucination faded, but not completely. The ice beneath her paws seemed to ripple like water.
Through the mist, she saw shapes—her sister, standing at the far end of the gorge.
"Why didn’t you save me, Kari?" the vision whispered.

Kari froze. The image of her sister flickered like broken glass, splitting into a dozen smaller
figures—each accusing her, each turning black and crumbling into Stain-like tendrils.
"No... this isn’t real," she muttered, forcing herself forward.
Her focus returned when she saw something tangible: faint trails of blood across the ice, as
though someone had crossed before her. She followed them, step by step, fighting the twisting
illusions.
At the far edge of the gorge, a figure appeared in the distance—Raaz. His silhouette was
unmistakable, limping but still proud. Kari stopped, their gazes locking for a single, loaded
moment. His expression was unreadable, but in his golden eyes there was something like
respect. Without a word, Raaz turned and disappeared into the pale mist.
Kari stood still, her breath clouding in the cold air. For the first time, she felt something other
than fear—something like a fragile thread of understanding.
If Kari abandoned Raaz:
The gorge was darker, the light muted as if swallowed by the mist. The colors of the Stain
burned brighter, veins of green and violet pulsing just beneath the surface. Every breath Kari
took filled her chest with a strange heaviness, and every shadow looked like it might come alive.
The hallucinations began almost instantly. She saw movement in the mist—shapes that looked
like her sister, walking alongside her.
"Why did you leave me, Kari?" the voice said, fragile and hollow. Kari’s steps faltered. The
figure reached for her, but when she blinked, it was gone.
A roar split the air, and suddenly, a monstrous version of Raaz appeared—twisted by the Stain.
His once-majestic body was warped with black spines and jagged patches of corrupted flesh.
His eyes burned like twin fires, fixed on her.
"Raaz...? No... no, this can’t be real," Kari whispered.
But the infected vision charged at them, forcing Kari and Whisker to run across the slippery ice.
The ground cracked beneath them, and the air was filled with whispers—voices that sounded
like both Raaz and her lost sister, accusing and distorted.
"You left me... You left us all..."
Whisker swooped in close, shouting, "Ignore it, Kari! Run!" His voice was the only thing
anchoring her to reality. Together they raced, dodging the phantom lynx’s claws, leaping over
fissures as the gorge bent and twisted in impossible ways.

When they finally reached the other side, Kari collapsed, trembling and breathless. Slowly, she
gathered stones from the frozen ground and built a small monument.
"For you, sister," she whispered, placing a small token at the top. She hesitated, then added
another stone. "And for you, Raaz... wherever you are. No matter what happened, the Stain is
the real enemy."
Whisker landed beside her, silent. He disapproved—she could see it in his eyes—but he didn’t
speak. Instead, he bowed his head slightly, a quiet gesture of respect for her grief.

New Character Profile – Human Survivor (Kenji)
Name: Kenji
Species: Human
Age: Late teens (17–18)
Gender: Male
Appearance:
Kenji is a lean but strong young man of East Asian descent, with short black hair and sharp,
intelligent eyes. He bears a faint scar across his right eyebrow—a reminder of a close call
during his youth while playing too close to the wilds. He wears a small, metallic pendant
shaped like a crescent fang , worn and dulled by time. This pendant is the key to identifying
him later in the quest.
Personality:
Kind, reserved, and deeply empathetic toward animals—especially Raaz, whom he once freed
from a hunter’s snare when he was just a child. Despite being raised to fear predators, Kenji
formed a brief bond with the lynx, feeding him secretly and even sharing a few moments of play
before they parted. That memory stayed with him, and he returned to the wilderness as a
volunteer helping a research group—but was separated during the outbreak.
Current State (Quest 1):
Kenji is found unconscious or barely conscious in the remains of an old cabin or shed,
hidden under a pile of tattered cloth or snow. His pendant is visible on his chest. Kari and
Whisker may find him before encountering Raaz , or after, depending on the player’s path
through the ruins.

Quest 2. “Echo of the Gorge” – Full narrative draft
The air in the gorge is heavy and humid. A cold wind blows through the chasm, carrying with it
dark blue dust. Each particle settles on Kari’s fur like an intangible cloak. Whisker flies
overhead, injured from the village, but even he sneezes and shakes his feathers.
“I don’t like this...” murmurs the owl, turning his head almost completely to look at the rock walls.
“These spores... they aren’t like snow dust. They are the Stain; they can cause visions and
disorient you.”
Kari takes a deep breath through her mouth, avoiding inhaling too much. The smell is harsh and
sweet, different from the metallic scent of the ruined areas where they had seen corruption
before. “So this dust makes us see things?” she whispers, recalling the warnings about
hallucinations and the aggression the infection causes in living beings.
“Yes. And I still don’t know whether that’s better or worse than what’s really here,” Whisker
replies.

Scenario B: You helped (or saved) Raaz and Kenji in Quest 1

Reflection / Evaluation 26 -08 - 2025
This experience has shown me that being a Narrative Designer is not just about writing—it is
about understanding how to integrate a story into the game itself. It means thinking about
how the world, the environment, and the gameplay mechanics can become vessels for
storytelling. It is about finding ways to connect with the player through in-game elements and
events that spark genuine emotion, rather than relying solely on dialogue or exposition.
I have learned that character design has a profound impact on how players perceive and
connect with the narrative. Most players do not stop to read long character descriptions; instead,
they discover personalities through gameplay interactions, behaviors, and consequences. This
insight has pushed me to focus on designing characters whose traits and values emerge
naturally during play.
The story I developed is both beautiful and tragic. On a personal level, it made me reflect on the
value of time, of life itself, and of the Earth we inhabit. It reminded me of the importance of
teamwork and building bonds, and of how sometimes setting aside differences can be the key to
survival.
Finally, I would like to express my gratitude to the entire FOQUES team for giving me the
opportunity to keep learning and for valuing my work as a Narrative Designer. I wish them the
very best for the future of the game and the studio.
